**[Title]** [Title up while you walk up.]

**[Psalm 107:2]** [Read Psalm 107:2.] “Let the redeemed of the LORD tell their story.” That’s what I’m here to do tonight.

**[Intro]** Hey guys, I’m Connor. I grew up in Maplewood, New Jersey, and now I live in Fairfax. Skylar and I met at Stevens, stayed friends for years, started dating in April 2024, and got married this May. I’ve got one brother, Spencer, out in California.

I started watching Grace online in May 2024, because Skylar had been coming here, and I came in person the next February. This June I became the Sunday Night Service Coordinator.

By day I’m a supply chain consultant at St. Onge. I help companies figure out how many facilities they need, how big, and where. Off the clock: ukulele, Minecraft, D&D, Star Wars, lifting, and time at Beaver Lake.

**[Jar, full screen]** Before I get into it, you need to know about the douchebag jar. If you’ve seen New Girl, you know how it works: say something that makes you sound like a douchebag, and you put a dollar in the jar.

**[Sex, drugs, rock and roll]** My story is all about sex, drugs, and rock and roll.

**[Sex, drugs + jar]** [Jar slide. Pause for the laugh.]

**[Age 4–5 · a song I already knew]** God was always real to me. I just spent about ten years running from him.

It started in my mom’s car. She’d play children’s hymns, songs like “I’ve Got the Joy.” One day I told her, “Mom, I have the love of Jesus in my heart.”

**[Age 8]** Growing up, I sang in the church choir and went to Sunday school.

But around eight, my family started to fracture. And around the same time, I saw graphic images, way too young.

**[Age 13 · what I told myself]** By thirteen, the divorce was final, and my dad started tearing down my mom. She was the one who’d given me my faith. When she got torn down, my faith went with her.

And I wanted sex. Porn was right there on a laptop, and every time it cost me shame. Everything around me said that’s how you grow up. I wanted to prove I was a big man.

At church, sex was a hard no, wrapped in shame. So at thirteen I stopped going with my mom, and I decided if God is so loving, he doesn’t care what I do. I wasn’t running from God. I was running from his authority.

Looking back, the shame didn’t stop anything. It just pushed God out.

**[Age 16]** By sixteen I was on every dating app there was, chasing casual sex, with unfortunate success. Instant gratification, and I was in charge.

**[Age 16 + jar]** [Jar slide. Pause for the laugh.]

**[Age 18 · met Skylar]** What I couldn’t see was that God was already working. At eighteen, I met Skylar at Stevens, and we became friends.

**[Age 19 · just friends]** At nineteen we went out, and she decided… okay, we decided we should just be friends.

**[Age 19 · numbing]** Around nineteen I started smoking weed, then vaping. Pretty soon it was just about numbing out.

By twenty-three, if you’d asked me who I was, I’d have said a cool guy who likes to have fun.

**[Age 21 · the pact]** Around twenty-one, after college, Skylar and I made a pact that we’d get married someday. We always argued about the age.

**[Pact + jar]** [Jar slide. Pause for the laugh.]

Years later, in therapy, I saw it for what it was. The pact and casual sex were both safety nets. They kept me from going after what I really wanted, which was a stable relationship.

**[Phone calls · letting go 1 of 3]** But Skylar and I never stopped talking. From October to April, we spent seventy-three hours on the phone. By March, it was twenty hours a month.

In March, I told her we should end the pact. No more safety net. That’s when she told me she’d been interested in me. We talked for over four hours that day.

She’d also told me her future husband would be a Christian. So yes, God got my attention through a girl. I know how that sounds.

God knew exactly how to reach me.

**[Amazing Grace · a song I already knew]** At twenty-four, Skylar and I started dating, and I went to Every Nation New Jersey. Pastor Adam opened every service the same way: “God has a word for you.” Then worship started, a big room full of people making music together. I’d spent years looking for that, even in a New Jersey chorus where I was the youngest by forty years.

Early on, maybe my very first Sunday, the song right after Pastor Adam was “Amazing Grace (My Chains Are Gone),” and my voice shook. I already knew that song.

**[God has a word for you]** And God did have a word for me. On purely secular terms, I’d already figured out that casual sex was holding me back. That was exactly what I’d grown up hearing God wanted for me. The two lined up. And I thought, maybe I actually know a thing or two about God’s character.

**[Baptism · letting go 2 of 3]** Then came baptism. I had the perfect plan. Beaver Lake. I was praying about it, and a fish bit me.

I took that as God saying don’t do what you want, do what I want. So I got baptized as soon as I could, the next month, July 2024, with Every Nation New Jersey at Hack’s house.

**[June 2025 · letting go 3 of 3]** And then the big one. June 2025. I went to get weed, and I felt completely out of control, ashamed, far from God, feeding an addiction. That’s when I realized I wanted him in control.

I haven’t smoked since. I quit nicotine in December. I’m still tempted. But by the grace of the Spirit, I’m clean. It took a while.

**[Psalm 139]** [Read Psalm 139:1–12 aloud.]

**[For you]** So guys, here’s what I want you to take home: I ran from God’s authority for ten years, and he still knew exactly how to reach me. For me, it was through a girl.

So how is he trying to reach you right now? That’s a hard question, because to answer it you have to know his character, what he’s like and how he moves.

At your table tonight, John’s question: what’s something you know is true and important, but tend to forget when life gets stressful or difficult, and what might help you remember it? For me, it’s simple. God is in control, and I keep trying to take it back. These days it’s my time. I still want to decide how every hour gets spent. Thanks, guys.

## Draft 1 (~730 words, about 5–6 min spoken, leaves room for the sing-along and laughs)

> Rough first pass in Connor's voice, built from his 10/15 notes. Bracketed lines are stage directions. Read it out loud and cut whatever doesn't sound like you.

Before I start, I need everybody to know about the douchebag jar. If you've seen *New Girl*, you know it: any time somebody says something that makes them sound like a douchebag, they put a dollar in the jar.

[pull out a dollar]

I'm putting mine in now, because part of my story is that God got my attention through a girl. I know how that sounds. Stay with me.

My theme tonight is pretty simple: **God knows you.** He knows you better than you know yourself, and he knows exactly what it'll take to reach you.

I'll start when I was really little. I remember telling my mom, totally serious, that I had the love of Jesus in my heart. I didn't make that up; I got it from a song. Some of you grew up singing it too.

[sing the first line of "I've Got the Joy" and let the room finish it]

That was me. Little kid, all in.

That didn't last. I was exposed to some really graphic stuff way too young, younger than any kid should be. Then, as I got older, everything I heard at church about sex and desire was a hard no, wrapped in a lot of shame. So I took the only lesson I thought was on offer: the stuff going on inside me was bad, so God must not want me. By the end of my childhood I'd walked away.

Here's what out of church actually looked like for me. From about 16 to 23, I was on every dating app there was, and if I'm honest, I was looking for casual sex. Along the way I picked up a marijuana habit, a nicotine habit, and for a short stretch, a cocaine habit.

*[Connor to confirm the bridge:]* Looking back, none of that was really what I was after.

Underneath all of it were two desires I couldn't shake. Here's the part I couldn't see then: even while I was gone, God was stirring them in me. I wanted real community, people around me who actually knew me. And I wanted to make music with a big group of people.

So I tried to build both of those myself.

First, I tried to organize my apartment building. Get-togethers, getting to know the neighbors. The leasing office shut it down. Turns out you can't just start a community in a building you rent.

Then I joined a chorus in New Jersey. Great idea, except I was the youngest guy there by about forty years, and half the music was in Italian. I didn't want to sing in Italian. So that didn't last either.

Two desires, two attempts, two dead ends.

[pause]

Then I met Skylar. Early on, she told me something that stopped me in my tracks: her future husband was going to be a Christian.

[beat]

Yeah. Dollar in the jar.

But I want to be honest about what happened next. I didn't run to church to win her over. That comment made me actually think, for the first time in years, about what being a Christian would mean, and whether the God I'd walked away from as a kid was really who I thought he was.

So I tried out my local church, Every Nation New Jersey.

The service started with worship. A big room full of people making music together, just like we do at Grace. The exact thing I'd been chasing in that chorus was happening in the first five minutes. Then, after the service, because I was a new face, several people came up to me. They were happy to meet me, and they made me feel welcome. The community I couldn't build in my apartment building was just there.

Both of the desires I'd been carrying around for years, the ones I kept trying to satisfy on my own, were met in one place.

That's when it hit me. God knew. He knew the shame that pushed me out. He knew those desires were in me, because he put them there. And he knew that the guy I was back then wasn't going to listen to a pastor, or a sermon, or a guy handing out tracts. But he would listen to Skylar.

**God will use whoever you'll actually listen to.**

For me, that was the woman who's now my wife. For you, it might be a buddy, a coworker, your kid, maybe somebody at your table tonight. It might be a desire you can't shake that keeps pointing you somewhere.

So here's what I'd ask you this week. Who's the voice in your life you actually listen to? Pay attention, because God might already be using it.

And if you're the voice for somebody else, take that seriously too.

Thanks, guys.
