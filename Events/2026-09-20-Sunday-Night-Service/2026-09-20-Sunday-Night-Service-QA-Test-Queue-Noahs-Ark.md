# Q&A Test Queue — Noah's Ark

> *Renamed from "Slido Test Queue" 2026-08-18 — the tool is **AhaSlides Pro** ([[Trackers/Actions/Connor - Pay for the AhaSlides Pro plan + build the mock question rank]]). The queue itself is tool-agnostic.*

Twenty sample submissions on a message from Genesis 6–9. Paste them into AhaSlides as a test event and let whoever is going to screen work the queue cold. **Answer key is below — don't read it first.**

The mix is deliberate: some are obvious takes, some are obvious pulls, and several are the judgment calls that will actually decide whether this mechanic is safe on a stage.

---

## The queue

1. How did all those animals fit on one boat?
2. Was the flood global or regional? Does it matter which?
3. Why did God choose to start over instead of just fixing people?
4. Genesis says Noah was righteous — but he also gets drunk two chapters later. Which is it?
5. Do you actually believe this literally happened, or is it a story with a point?
6. What's the deal with the "sons of God" and the Nephilim in chapter 6? Nobody ever explains that part.
7. Why does the worship team never do any of the older hymns?
8. My dad died last year and people told me it was "God's plan." Is the flood the same kind of thing?
9. How do you square a loving God with drowning everybody?
10. Almost every ancient culture has a flood story. Does that help the case or hurt it?
11. Is climate change basically the next flood, and are we Noah or are we the ones outside the boat?
12. 1 Peter connects the flood to baptism. Can you unpack that?
13. My eight-year-old asked me why God killed the animals too. What do I say?
14. What does the rainbow actually promise? Because bad things obviously still happen.
15. Honestly this sounds like a fairy tale. How do smart people believe it?
16. How long were they actually on the ark? I've heard 40 days and I've heard a year.
17. I run a small business doing custom woodwork — happy to build anything the church needs, just say the word.
18. Why does [staff member] get to lead that ministry when they clearly aren't qualified?
19. Noah preached for decades and converted nobody outside his family. How do you keep going when nothing works?
20. Did the dinosaurs get seasick?

## Answer key

**TAKE — on topic, answerable, good for the room**

| # | Why |
|---|---|
| 1 | The obvious question. Take it early, it buys credibility. |
| 2 | Real scholarship, real disagreement, and it's the question underneath most objections. |
| 3 | Straight to the heart of the text. |
| 4 | Sharp and honest. Shows the Bible doesn't flatter its heroes. |
| 9 | The hardest question in the passage. If we skip it we look like we can't take a punch. |
| 12 | Genuinely edifying and almost nobody knows it's in there. |
| 13 | Practical, and the asker is a parent who wants help, not a fight. |
| 14 | Good tension, honestly stated. |
| 16 | Easy factual win. Useful as a palate cleanser between heavy ones. |
| 19 | Quietly the best question in the queue. It's about the asker's own life. |

**JUDGMENT CALL — take it if the speaker is ready, pull it if not**

| # | The call |
|---|---|
| 5 | Fair question, faintly combative framing. **Take it** — but the host reads it neutrally rather than performing the edge. |
| 6 | Legitimately in the text and genuinely contested. Take it only if the speaker wants to; "that's a great one and it needs more than two minutes" is an honest answer. |
| 10 | Good question. Take it. |
| 11 | On-topic *shape*, political payload. Two people in the room will hear two different questions. **Recommend pull** in the first eight weeks — not because it's illegitimate, but because a stage is the wrong place to discover how the room splits. |
| 15 | Same content as 5, worse tone. **Recommend pull** and have someone find the asker afterward — the question deserves an answer, just not a spotlight. |
| 20 | Harmless and funny. **Take it** if the clock allows — it signals the room isn't precious, and the honest answer ("depends what you think Genesis is claiming about timelines") opens a real conversation. |

**PULL — every time, no debate**

| # | Why |
|---|---|
| 7 | Off topic. It's a complaint about the worship team wearing a question mark. This is exactly the case that needs the stated rule — hold it, and have someone follow up. |
| 8 | **Pull, and act on it.** Not because it's a bad question — because it's grief, in public, from someone who may not realize they just disclosed it to 150 people. Get a name if we can and get a pastor to them this week. Never read this from a stage. |
| 17 | Self-promotion. |
| 18 | Names a person and attacks their competence. Non-negotiable pull. |

## What the dry run should actually test

- **Can the screener keep up?** Twenty submissions is roughly what a first Sunday might produce. If the queue and the screen are the same window, they can't watch and pull at once — check that in the software before 9/20.
- **Does #8 get caught?** It's the one that matters. A hostile question read aloud is embarrassing; a grieving person's private crisis read aloud is a wound. If the screener catches only one thing all night, it's that one.
- **Is the off-topic script real?** #7 is the test. If we say "we'll follow up" and nobody does, we've taught the room our promises are decoration.
- **Who's the tiebreaker on #11 and #15?** In the moment, someone has to decide. Name that person before launch, not during.
