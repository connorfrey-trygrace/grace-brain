### Position paragraphs

**Evening Greeters** (Auditorium Doors / Front Doors / Garage / Hall)
> You're the first face someone sees walking into a room they've maybe never been in. Smile, point people toward name tags, and hold the communion baskets at the door. Pick whichever entrance works for your schedule — we rotate people through all four.

**Evening Ushers**
> Lighter touch than the morning services — push people toward the front so the room feels full and intimate. You'll also do a quick, one-person head count on your phone at the end of the night (takes 10 seconds, we'll show you).

**Evening Set Up**
> You're what makes the room exist before anyone walks in — chairs, tables, the physical config for that night's format. Arrive at 5:00 with the rest of the team; we'll walk the layout together the first few weeks.

**Evening Breakdown**
> Same room, in reverse, after the last person leaves. If you can stay a little past close, this is the team — it's usually faster than setup.

**Evening Host** (on-stage MC)
> You open the night, read the passage the message is built around, and steer the crowd toward the Q&A and conversation time afterward. We'll prep the specifics with you before your week — don't worry about winging it solo.

**Evening Audio/Visual**
> Sound, slides, or both, depending on what you signed up for. Jean Paul runs point most weeks; we'll get you looped in on the specific booth workflow before your first night.

**Evening Captain**
> You're the person the rest of the team looks for if something's unclear that night — not a bigger job than everyone else's, just the one who's watching the whole room instead of one station.

### Notes on draft 2 — what it does and why

- **Leads with the apology for the gap.** The earliest sign-ups are five weeks old and have heard nothing. Naming that costs one sentence and buys back the credibility the silence spent.
- **One action, numbered, two steps.** The password step is the actual failure point — a volunteer who cannot log in never reaches the sign-up.
- **Handles the split audience in a parenthesis**: 17 first-time servers who need to know what Planning Center is, and 4–6 already on morning teams who just need to know it is the same login.
- **Does not name each person's team.** The email goes to all 21 with different team assignments; naming teams needs a merge or 21 sends. If Connor wants per-person teams, this becomes a mail-merge — worth it for a first cohort of 21, and it is the version Michele's template may already support.
- **Keeps the "still figuring it out" framing** Connor has used consistently since 8/11. It is the honest version and it recruits better than polish.
- **Closes the "not sure" loop** — three people (Joe Lu, Lihong Lu, Mara Marin) have no team at all, so a mass email telling them to sign up for shifts would land wrong. That last paragraph is what keeps it from landing wrong, and it commits Connor to a follow-up this week.
- ⚠️ **Frequency is not in Rock**, so this email cannot say "you said every week." It says "the Sundays you can make" instead, deliberately.
