## Why these seven

- **Q2 is one question, not four.** A Forms grid keeps it to a single screen and a single tap-column, and it scores all four parts of the night on the same scale — so you can see whether a weak week was the seating, the Q&A, or the message. It also stays accurate when the order flips at week 5, which a question worded "afterward" would not.
- **Q3** is the whole thesis of the service in one question. Track it as the percentage answering "Yes" and watch it move by format.
- **Q4** exists because the Q&A now runs all eight weeks. It tells you the mechanic's actual *reach* — if "I didn't know I could" is high, the problem is the announcement, not the questions.
- **Q5** exists because "people not knowing what to do" was the loudest complaint from the NYC trip. If it doesn't stay above ~80% "clear the whole time," the format is too clever.
- **Q6** is your retention signal, most useful filtered to Q1 = "First time."
- **Q1** is what makes the rest readable. A 3.2 from first-timers means something very different from a 3.2 from regulars.

## What to leave off

Nothing about demographics, giving, volunteering, or "how did you hear about us." Every added question costs completion rate, and a 20% response rate on seven good questions beats 6% on fourteen.

## Weekly rhythm

Pull results Monday morning. Log five numbers into one running sheet — attendance, stay rate, the Q&A average, the conversation-time average, and the "Yes" percentage on Q3. That sheet is what you and John look at in week 8. Push the aggregate into Rock later if it's useful; never person-by-person.
