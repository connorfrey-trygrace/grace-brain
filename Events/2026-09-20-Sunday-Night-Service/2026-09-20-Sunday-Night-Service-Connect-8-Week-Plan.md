### The Q&A is a constant, not an experiment

**There is a Q&A all eight weeks.** People text questions in during the service and vote them up; we answer the top ones that night. That is the one thing everyone who went to St. Joe's said to keep, and we are not going to test whether to have it. What we test is **where it sits** — before the room talks, or after, fed by what the room actually argued about. Whether it stays at all is a question for later, once we know what it's worth.

> [!success] Q&A mechanics — locked 2026-08-12
> - **No spontaneously answered questions, weeks 1–8.** The night's question is known in advance and **the staff works out how to articulate the answer during the week.** This is not caution for its own sake: John reported a staff member on 8/11 saying flatly *"Hey, no, I'm not doing that"* about answering cold from a stage. Preparation is what makes the roster of possible responders bigger than one.
> - **One question of the night**, voted to the top the previous Sunday, teased ahead as the reason to come. (For 9/20 it comes from the 9/13 morning service.)
> - **Live submission still happens** — the queue goes up on the screens during the transition out of the message, which is when the most questions arrive. That window is *"a fun time, a curious time, an intriguing time"* (John) and the host pair **vamps off the questions rising up the board**, reading them out and reacting. Derek's own story is the reasoning: the church that got him in his 20s never answered his questions, but it **read them out loud**, and being acknowledged was the thing that worked.
> - **The host nuances the question and hands it to the responder.** The asker is not on stage, so the host has to put some of themselves into it — *"I wonder if these persons who voted this to the top are wondering this."*
> - **Screening happens in the booth, never from the host chair.** The host pair cannot screen and vamp at the same time. ✅ **Assigned 2026-08-22 — Michael Vacca**, in the ProPresenter seat, accepted; screening stacks onto ProPresenter because the Q&A runs after the message. His condition: **he does not pick the question** — he passes or kills each submission as it arrives and edits out what should not reach the screen. Jean Paul Tauler ruled out 8/17 on capacity. **Top 5–6 up-voted questions reach the big screens** (8/12 said ~10); the rest stay on phones. Source: [[Transcripts/2026-08-22 15.04 Connor–Michael Vacca Call — Screener Seat Accepted & 8-30 Lunch Confirmed]].
> - **Honest limit, stated:** *"I think that we have the ability to monitor that very closely. I do not think that we have the ability to completely filter it out."* The realistic exposure is slurs and deliberate offense, not sex questions; only the top ~10 surface, and getting there takes votes, which is itself a filter.
> - **Host pairs are approved in principle** and need not be fixed forever — John floated designated pairs, Connor floated rotating them (a ladies' week was his example). ⚠️ **No first pair was picked and no date was set for the ask.** The agenda's decision #5 did not land. **Update 2026-09-02 (text):** the first pair *was* picked — John Slye texted Connor 9/1 8:32 AM ET, *"Hosts confirmed — Sept 20- Emily & Simba, Sept 27- Will & Brandon."* Emily Horneffer & Simba Kanjanda for launch night, Will Gralley & Brandon Geddes for week 2. See [[Events/2026-09-20-Sunday-Night-Service/2026-09-20-Sunday-Night-Service-Role-Ask-List]] for the corroborating Simba thread.
> - **Follow-up questions do not get answered from the front — they become the connect conversation.** *(added 2026-08-17)* No live follow-ups, and no stage-side circle after dismissal either; Connor was *"reluctant to even mention"* the opportunity exists. Instead the **top questions stay up on the screen during connect time** and the room talks about them. John: *"rather than the talking head up front somehow containing all the answers… we can all do this together."* Prompts may draw on the message, the QOTN, or future QOTNs — *"we don't need to tell people what to talk about, but we can certainly give them ideas."* **This is a real change to what connect time is prompted from** — the prior design anchored discussion to the *message*.
> - **The banter sparks next week's question; it does not select it.** *(added 2026-08-17)* Reframed from *"let's use this time to find the question for next week"* to *"let's observe what some of the questions may have been this week to spark the questions next week, and please continue to submit your questions throughout the night."* Submission stays open all night.
> - **The voted question is not announced from the stage.** *(added 2026-08-17)* It resolves at midnight and goes out midweek — Monday or Tuesday — on John's broadcast list. *"We don't need to say as people are leaving, by the way, here's the question next week."*
> - **QOTN answer length reconfirmed at 10-12 minutes** *(2026-08-17)*.
> - **Tool decided 2026-08-18 (Connor): AhaSlides Pro**, ~$15.95/mo, *"unlimited audience size"*. **Purchased 2026-08-18, annual, $191.40.** **This supersedes the 8/12 call's tier reasoning** — that discussion ran on Slido's ladder (sub-300 now, $150/mo enterprise for 5,000, reevaluate at 300; John on the 5,000 ceiling: *"Oh my gosh, that's good."*). On AhaSlides there is no ladder and no attendance trigger: one plan, no cap. ≈$1,600/yr cheaper. Open caveat: *"unlimited"* needs written confirmation, since it is now the whole capacity case.


Three other things run every week, weeks 1–8:

1. **"Tonight's plan" on the screen** before service and again before the message ends. The #1 complaint from the NYC trip was people not knowing what was next — and since the order changes at week 5, this matters more here than it would in a fixed format. We fix it on night one and never stop.
2. **The experiment is announced from the front.** "We're trying something new for the next eight weeks. Tell us how it went." People forgive a lot when you tell them it's a trial.
3. **QR code for the feedback form** — on the screen and on the chair backs. Same questions every week.

**Not changing in the first 8 weeks:** no food, and no assigned or recurring table seating. Those are the second experiment, not this one.

## Two questions, eight weeks

We are testing exactly two things:

1. **Breakout rooms, or one room?** — when people are offered somewhere else to go, does a meaningful slice take it, and do they connect better when they do?
2. **Discussion before the Q&A, or after?** — does the room talk first, or watch the QOTN get answered and then talk?

Two variables, two settings each, two Sundays per combination. Every combination gets run.

| | **Discussion after the Q&A** | **Discussion before the Q&A** |
|---|---|---|
| **One room** | **Weeks 1–2** · Sept 20 + 27 ⭐ | **Weeks 7–8** · Nov 1 + 8 |
| **Breakout rooms** | **Weeks 3–4** · Oct 4 + 11 ⭐ | **Weeks 5–6** · Oct 18 + 25 |

> [!important] ⭐ **Location axis flipped 2026-08-30 — auditorium first.** Weeks 1–2 and weeks 3–4 swapped cells; weeks 5–8 are untouched. **Decided in the formation lunch: Connor proposed it, John Slye endorsed it, nothing else was restated.** Connor: *"maybe it makes sense to start those first 2 weeks of the experiment with the peer discussion happening in the auditorium, just so that we have a sense for how many people are there. So that when we do do breakout rooms, we know how many we need."* John: *"what that does is it ensures that we have kind of take a pulse first… once we take that another course of action, it's kind of hard to back out of it. Your approach allows for flexibility and adjustment without there being too much pain involved."* The 2×2 is intact — every combination still runs two Sundays and each question still answers off four. Source: [[Transcripts/2026-08-30 13.10 Sunday Night Formation Lunch — Team Assembled, Captain Roles Unfilled]]

> [!important] Rooms are opt-in on the spot *(Connor, 2026-08-17)* — this is what makes the grid work
> Nobody is assigned, nothing is pre-sorted, and no capacity has to be forecast. The host names the rooms at dismissal and people walk or don't. **Two named leaders: Brandon Geddes and Simba Kanjanda**, one per room.
>
> **Hosting and room-leading do not collide** *(Connor, 2026-08-17)*. **Hosting happens before the breakout rooms** — welcome, mood, banter, and the handoff into the QOTN all sit ahead of connect time, never during it. A host can lead a room the same night. The one narrow constraint: in weeks 5–8 the QOTN closes the night, so whoever is handing it to the responder has to be out of their room and back up front before the rooms return.
>
> This is the reason rooms sit in the launch weeks where tables could not. Tables had to be physically set before doors against an unknown headcount of 180–250 — that is what forced them out on capacity grounds and what would have forced rooms into the back half of the season, confounding the rooms question with time. Opt-in rooms have neither problem: they cost two leaders and a slide, and they scale to whoever shows up. **John's ~25% estimate is the design target, not a cap** — if 25% of 200 walk, that is ~50 people across two rooms.

## What we count

Four numbers, every week, no exceptions:

| Metric | How | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Attendance** | Usher count, QR to Rock | Denominator for everything else |
| **Stay rate** | Headcount 15 min after dismissal ÷ attendance | The single best measure of whether Connect worked |
| **Feedback** | **Rock connect card**, QR, **not anonymous** — `trygrace.org/sundaynightserviceconnectcard`, results in Rock report 219 *(corrected 2026-08-23; the Google Form was cancelled 8/19)* | The "how did it feel" side |
| **Team read** | 3-line note from Connor + host, same night | Catches what numbers miss |
| **Off-ramp rate** *(added 2026-08-17)* | People who took a named next step ÷ attendance | Stay-rate says the night was warm; this says anyone is still connected on Tuesday |

> [!important] Connect time is not one of the variables — it is the thing itself *(Connor, 2026-08-17)*
> Asked what happens if connect time underperforms, given that killing live follow-up questions routes every unanswered question into it: *"Live follow up is too much of a risk. If connection time underperforms, we will have to rework it until it performs well. The goal is connection time, if it fails, we fail."*
>
> **Read the eight weeks accordingly.** The experiment tests *how* connect time is shaped — rooms or no rooms, before or after the Q&A. It does not test *whether* to have it. A bad result is a redesign instruction, not an exit. The dependency created on 8/17 — follow-up questions have no other home — is therefore accepted deliberately, not overlooked.

**The bar:** it's working if **half the room is still there 15 minutes after dismissal** and most people say they had a real conversation with someone they didn't know.

> [!warning] Off-ramp rate will read **0** on night one, and that is the point *(added 2026-08-17, Connor's 6th-bucket call)*
> The three things that would move it — assigned seating, consistent groups, tap-to-connect — are all out of weeks 1–8, and the small-groups handoff to Alexis Hendrickson is date-locked to **12/1**, three weeks *after* this run ends. So a person who has the exact conversation this design is built to produce has nothing named to walk toward.
>
> **A night can score 60% stay-rate and 0% off-ramp.** That is a different failure with a different fix, and averaging it into stay-rate hides it until December. Recording the zero every week is what keeps it visible. → [[Archive/Actions/Connor - Name the in-room next step for Sunday night weeks 1-8 (off-ramp bucket)]]

### How to read it at the end

Because every combination gets run, each question gets answered off **four Sundays, not two**:

- **Do rooms matter?** Weeks 1–2 + 5–6 (rooms) against weeks 3–4 + 7–8 (one room).
- **Does order matter?** Weeks 1–2 + 3–4 (Q&A first) against weeks 5–6 + 7–8 (talk first).

**One extra number on rooms weeks:** the **take-up rate** — how many of the room actually walked into a breakout, against John's ~25% estimate. Stay-rate alone can't distinguish "rooms worked" from "nobody used them," and those need different fixes.

That's the real advantage of running it this way rather than as four unrelated formats — the same eight Sundays answer two questions instead of comparing four things to each other and settling nothing.

**Fair warning on the data — and the 8/30 swap made this worse, not better:** weeks 1–2 are launch weeks, so the crowd is curious rather than typical. **Oct 4** has a guest worship lead (Rhett Walker, solo acoustic in the evening); **Oct 11** has Seth Addison away with worship unconfirmed; **Nov 1** is an expected low-turnout Sunday. ⚠️ **Under the old grid every noisy Sunday sat in a different cell. Under the new one, Oct 4 and Oct 11 are the same cell — weeks 3–4, the first rooms weeks.** Both of the noisiest Sundays now land together on the arm the whole rooms question turns on. Rooms still get four Sundays (Oct 4, 11, 18, 25), so the comparison survives, but **weight Oct 18 + 25 more heavily than Oct 4 + 11 when reading the rooms answer.**

## What we are deliberately not testing yet

**Food.** Probably the biggest single lever on whether people stay, which is exactly why it can't go in now — drop pizza into week 6 and we'll never know whether the tables worked or the pizza did. **Food comes in the second run, after the rooms question**, and it will need an owner: Derek closed the door on Felipe catering it (8/11), so there is currently nobody to run it.

**Parallel rooms at different depths.** **Overturned 2026-08-12** (was: "good idea, wrong order — week 9") — rooms are now the *first* variable, in place of tables, because the room may not physically hold tables. John's caveat, and it is the right one: *"we can't fit 500 people. 300 people"* in breakout rooms either — but neither he nor Connor expects most of the room to want a group. John's estimate: **~85% stay for the Q&A, ~25% go into a group**, because a group turns the night into a **2.5-hour commitment** and *"I only think 25% of the people are gonna be willing to give it."* That 25% is the design target. **Resolved 2026-08-17: rooms are opt-in on the spot, led by Brandon Geddes and Simba Kanjanda** *(name corrected — see the Launch note's 2026-08-22 correction; "two of them" is now sized off the weeks 1–2 stay-rate per the 8/30 swap)* — so the capacity ceiling John named never binds, because the rooms are sized by whoever chooses to walk in.

**Mingle-before vs. mingle-after** — arrival, not connect. Two variables is already the most eight Sundays can honestly carry.

**The two-day question arc** — posing the question Sunday morning and answering it at 6 PM. **Partly overturned 2026-08-12.** It does not run weekly — Connor's objection held on that. It runs **once, on 9/13**, as the launch seed, for the specific reason that 9/13 is the one Sunday night that cannot happen. John's added kicker, which is what makes it more than a morning-crowd play: he will push the question out on his **~1,200-person broadcast text** between the 13th and the 20th with a live-or-YouTube invite, which reaches people who were never in the room at 9 AM. That is the "salvage" version the agenda itself proposed, arrived at from the other direction.

**Whether to keep the Q&A at all.** Deliberately off the table until Nov 8. We keep it, we learn what it's worth, and *then* we ask.
