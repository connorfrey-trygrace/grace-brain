## Findings

### 1. A real, uninvestigated gap: the 9/11 weekly-synthesis run appears not to have fired — and the vault caught this by accident, not by design (Ash, Karpathy, Austin) — Tier 1 follow-up + Tier 2 proposal

**Evidence:** `/Synthesis/` contains 14 dated notes, the most recent being `Weekly 2026-09-04.md`. 2026-09-11 was a Friday — `weekly-synthesis`'s cron (`0 16 * * 5`) should have fired, and per [[Workflows/weekly-synthesis]] Step 1 it must "still produce the synthesis" even with zero qualifying transcripts. No `Weekly 2026-09-11.md` exists anywhere in the vault (checked, not just `/Synthesis/` — nothing under `/Archive` either). This is not my inference alone: **`Agendas/2026-09-14 Connor–Derek Bi-weekly Sync Agenda.md`'s own `source` frontmatter field says it outright** — *"No weekly synthesis note exists past [[Synthesis/Weekly 2026-09-04]] (the 9/11 Friday run appears not to have fired)."* Nobody opened an investigation item for it the way [[Workflows/m365-run-log-health-check]] would for a suspected missed m365 run — because that watchdog pattern only ever lived inside `grace-weekly-refresh`, which was retired 2026-09-12, the day before this would have been checked on its weekly cadence.

**Why it matters:** `weekly-synthesis` is the one job in [[Trackers/Automation]] whose cadence *nothing* retired — it's still marked ✅ Enabled, still cronned Friday 4:09 PM. This is exactly the case Karpathy's "never delegate what you can't verify" and Austin's "receipt" concept exist for: a scheduled job silently produced no receipt, and the only reason anyone knows is that an unrelated agenda-drafting session happened to notice the absence three days later. If that agenda hadn't been drafted, this would still be invisible.

**Proposed change:** (Tier 1, mechanical) Confirm with Connor whether 9/11's run genuinely didn't fire (check `~/Claude/Scheduled/weekly-synthesis` run history if visible from his side) — if data supports it, backfill `Weekly 2026-09-11.md` retroactively from the aging report + the one transcript that existed that week; if the run did fire but errored before writing, that's a different, also-real defect. (Tier 2) Give `weekly-synthesis` its own receipt check the way the m365 sync has one — see Finding 4.

### 2. Retiring `grace-weekly-refresh` removed the only mechanism that would likely have caught Finding 1 quickly — the design tradeoff is real, not hypothetical anymore (Austin vs. Karpathy; Stanley) — decision for Connor

**Evidence:** Before 2026-09-12, the scheduler heartbeat (`grace-weekly-refresh`'s weekly-only duty, per its §Weekly-only duties) caught exactly this class of drift — `Changelog/_history/Automation-changelog.md`'s 2026-08-10 entry shows it correctly noticing `weekly-synthesis`'s `lastRunAt` landed on a *Sunday* instead of a Friday (explained as Connor's laptop being asleep during vacation) and explicitly stating the reasoning that would apply again now: *"these tasks depend on the laptop being awake, so a run can silently slide by days without any error surfacing... If Friday runs keep landing on other days while Connor is at his desk, that's a different problem."* [[Logs/refresh-run-log]]'s 2026-08-17 entry shows the same heartbeat reporting "all five Grace tasks within cadence" as routine, boring, working infrastructure. That mechanism is now gone, and Finding 1 is the first real miss since it was retired.

**Why it matters, and why it's contested:** Austin and Boris would defend the retirement on its own terms — Connor's own call, heavy quick-refresh usage (9 runs in 6 days) means the trackers *feel* current, and per Austin's cautionary tales, an unused safety net isn't automatically worth keeping just because it once worked. But `grace-quick-refresh` **does not run `weekly-synthesis` or `derek-weekly-sync-agenda` at all** — they're separate scheduled tasks outside its four phases — so heavy quick-refresh usage doesn't actually compensate for either going dark, and Finding 1 shows that gap isn't theoretical. Karpathy and Ash read this as a leash loosened without a replacement, at the exact moment a miss occurred. Stanley's framing sharpens it further: the retired heartbeat was Four-Levels "Informed Progress" evidence — it had a working track record (the 8/10 catch above) — and retiring it outright is a step *backward* to zero oversight, not a graduated move to a higher trust level with lighter-touch verification. See Where the panel disagrees.

### 3. `derek-weekly-sync-agenda`'s cron no longer matches the real meeting cadence — but the system caught its own drift correctly; it's waiting on Connor, not broken (Cat Wu, Jeff Su) — Tier 2, mostly self-resolving

**Evidence:** [[Trackers/Automation]] still lists `derek-weekly-sync-agenda` as Tuesday 2:00 PM (`0 14 * * 2`). But `Agendas/` shows the last two entries are `2026-09-09 Connor–Derek Weekly Sync Agenda.md` (a Wednesday) and `2026-09-14 Connor–Derek Bi-weekly Sync Agenda.md` (a Monday) — no Tuesday agenda since 2026-09-01. The 9/14 note's own item 5 states the situation plainly: *"Connor's move to bi-weekly was a calendar-invite acceptance, never an explicit reply to Derek. The agenda-drafting automation... is deliberately still cronned for Tuesdays pending that confirmation."*

**Why it's mostly good news:** this is Jeff Su's Review phase and Cat Wu's "is the cadence earned by the actual rate of change" both working as intended — the system noticed its own automation no longer matches reality and queued a one-line human decision instead of drifting silently or guessing. It stands in useful contrast to Finding 1, where nothing noticed at all.

**Proposed change:** once Derek gives the spoken confirmation (already queued as agenda item 5), re-cron the task to the new day/cadence and update [[Trackers/Automation]]'s row in the same pass, per that file's own standing rule ("update this file whenever a schedule changes").

### 4. `system-audit.md`'s own weekly "Watcher check" still names the retired job, which makes it circular exactly where it matters most (Ash, Karpathy) — Tier 2

**Evidence:** [[Workflows/system-audit]] light-check item 9 reads: *"confirm `grace-weekly-refresh` actually ran on its weekly cadence... This job now holds the watchdog duties... If it silently dies, all gap detection goes dark at once, and this weekly check is the only thing positioned to notice."* This predates the 2026-09-12 retirement and was not touched by the 2026-09-15 deep audit (which fixed CLAUDE.md, `event-checklist-autocopy.md`, and `Welcome.md`, but not this file). This check runs *inside* `weekly-synthesis`'s own Friday pass — the same job Finding 1 shows may have gone dark. So the one mechanism meant to notice a miss is written to check a job that no longer exists, embedded in the job that might be the actual failure.

**Proposed change:** rewrite item 9 to drop the `grace-weekly-refresh` reference and instead (a) confirm last Friday's `Synthesis/Weekly` note exists, and (b) fold in a minimal scheduler heartbeat over the three still-live Grace cadences (`weekly-synthesis`, `derek-weekly-sync-agenda`, `persona-refresh-quarterly-sweep`) against [[Trackers/Automation]] — since nothing else runs one anymore. This is the same "extend what exists, don't add a job" move the 2026-07-16 audit used for `daily-stale-sweep`.

## Where the panel disagrees

- **Austin vs. Karpathy (Finding 2).** Austin: Connor's own call, and per the cautionary tales, keeping automation alive "just in case" is its own failure mode — the fix is to judge the retirement on whether it's actually costing something, not on principle. Karpathy: it *is* costing something now, concretely — Finding 1 is a real miss the old heartbeat's discipline would likely have caught within a week (per its own design threshold, 8 days), and it instead sat undetected until an unrelated agenda draft stumbled onto it. The panel doesn't resolve this — it's Stanley's "tension to manage," not a problem with a single fix — but both agree the *specific* proposal in Finding 4 (fold a minimal heartbeat into `weekly-synthesis` itself, no new scheduled job) satisfies Connor's own on-demand-over-automatic preference while closing the concrete gap Finding 1 exposed.
- **Cat vs. Dylan (Findings 2, 4).** Cat wants the heartbeat restored in some form — information should come to Connor, not wait for an accident like the 9/14 agenda catch. Dylan pushes back with the ROI question Automation.md still doesn't track: how much did the missing 9/11 synthesis actually cost Connor, given nothing broke and grace-quick-refresh kept the trackers current in the meantime? Both would accept Finding 4's proposal (a few lines inside an existing weekly job) as cheap enough to not need the ROI debate — but Dylan explicitly would not accept re-adding `grace-weekly-refresh` itself as a scheduled job to solve this.
- **Slye vs. the rest, lightly.** Slye's question — is the system optimizing to *look* current (nine quick-refresh runs, clean token logs) rather than *being* current underneath (a silently dark weekly-synthesis job) — is the same gap Finding 1 documents from a design-quality angle. He'd frame it as the panel's own subject matter proving his point rather than as a competing view; not a resolvable disagreement, just a different vocabulary for Finding 1.
