## 1 · The plan, in five minutes (0:00–0:10)

Walk [[Events/2026-09-20-Sunday-Night-Service/2026-09-20-Sunday-Night-Service-Connect-8-Week-Plan|the 8-week plan]]. The short version:

- **The spine never moves:** acoustic worship → the morning's message → **Q&A and conversation, both, every week** → room open till 8:00.
- **We are testing exactly two things, not eight ideas:**
  1. **Tables, or no tables?** Does the furniture earn the labor of setting it every week?
  2. **Does the room talk before the Q&A, or after?** Does the conversation feed the questions, or follow the answers?
- **Every combination runs two Sundays:**

| | **Q&A, then talk** | **Talk, then Q&A** |
|---|---|---|
| **Tables** | Weeks 1–2 · Sept 20 + 27 | Weeks 5–6 · Oct 18 + 25 |
| **No tables** | Weeks 3–4 · Oct 4 + 11 | Weeks 7–8 · Nov 1 + 8 |

- **Which means each question gets answered off four Sundays, not two** — tables is weeks 1–2 + 5–6 against 3–4 + 7–8; order is 1–2 + 3–4 against 5–6 + 7–8. That's the reason to run it this way instead of trying four unrelated formats and settling nothing.
- **No tables on Oct 4 and Oct 11 on purpose** — guest worship lead one week, no confirmed leader the other. The zero-setup combination belongs on the two shakiest Sundays.
- **We say it's an experiment, out loud, every week**, and we hand out a QR to a 7-question anonymous form.
- **The bar:** half the room still there fifteen minutes after dismissal.
- **Week 9 (Nov 15) is where parallel rooms go** — Go Deeper / Talk It Out / Just Hang. It's a third variable and it only makes sense once we know where the Q&A belongs.

**Ask:** two variables over eight weeks, or does John want more ideas tried faster? The tradeoff is real — more ideas means no clean answer on any of them.

## 2 · The Q&A, walked through (0:10–0:20)

The Q&A runs all eight weeks. It's the one thing every person who went to St. Joe's said to keep, so it's worth ten minutes on the mechanics rather than assuming we both mean the same thing.

**Who does what:**

- **The speaker answers. Every week, all eight.** That never moves.
- **The host runs the room** — reads the questions out, keeps the clock, moves it along — so the speaker only has to answer, not emcee. Per 7/22. Weeks 1–2 the speaker does it with one other person alongside; from week 3 the host pair takes it.
- **A screener watches the queue live** and can pull anything before it hits the screen. **Nobody owns this yet.**
- Staging: two seats down front rather than a podium. Cosmetic, not a decision.

**The tool is Slido.** Two things still to settle: which tier, and therefore what attendance we're planning for. Slido's tiers cap at 100 free / 200 / 300 / 5,000. The old analysis assumed **500+**, which forced the ~$150/mo Enterprise plan. **If Sunday night is realistically 100–150, the ~$12.50/mo tier covers it and the cost problem disappears.** Needs paying for and testing on the auditorium screen by **Sept 7**.

### Four questions to answer today

**① How much prep does the speaker get?**
This is really "when does the queue close." Options: at the start of the message (the speaker sees them while preaching — not real prep); at the end of worship (~30 min); or the night before, from a queue open all week. The answer depends on what John wants — the freshness of a live room, or a considered answer. It changes the whole submission window, so it can't stay vague.

**② Are we taking any live questions — or is the queue frozen once the Q&A starts?**
Frozen protects the speaker and lets the screener do their job properly. Live keeps it responsive and lets someone ask the obvious follow-up. **Recommend frozen for weeks 1–2 while we learn the room**, then revisit.

**③ How do we squash a question about a different topic?**
Needs a rule the host can say out loud without it feeling like censorship. Suggested: *"We're keeping tonight on what we heard in the message — if yours didn't get asked, it's still in the queue and someone will find you afterward."* Then it needs to be true: the screener keeps the off-topic ones, and somebody actually follows up. Otherwise the promise costs us more than the question would have.

**④ Live-only, or live plus one carried over?**
The 7/22 meeting locked the top question being answered **the following week**, not live. The plan answers them live. **The middle version:** answer as many as the clock allows tonight, and take the single best one we couldn't do justice to — next week opens with *"last week somebody asked this."* One question, one week of notice for the speaker, and a thread between Sundays that only involves people who were in the room on Sunday **night**.

## 3 · Responses to John's emails (0:20–0:45)

### ① Question of the Night — posed in the morning, answered at night
> *"Introduce the question Sunday morning, let people submit and vote all day, but don't answer it until Sunday night — **almost creating a reason to come back**."*

**Recommend: not in the first eight weeks — and I want to push back on the reasoning, not just the timing.**

The mechanic is clever and the craft is real. But read John's own words for what it's *for*: a reason to come back. That is a repeat-attendance lever, and it runs off the Sunday **morning** platform, which means the people it can reach are — definitionally — people who were already at Grace that morning.

**That is the opposite of what we said this service is for.** Sunday night was scoped to *complement, not cannibalize* Sunday morning. It exists for people who are not in the room at 9 AM: the 20s and 30s who don't do Sunday mornings, the people who need something slower, the ones for whom 6 PM is the only version of church that fits. If our best engagement idea only works on people already sitting in the morning service, we've built a mechanic that grows attendance without reaching anybody new.

**And John's own better sentence, from the same email, argues against it:**
> *"I really want Sunday night to naturally help people move toward relationships and community, not just attend another service."*

The arc is an **attend-another-service** mechanic. It's an attendance idea wearing a community idea's clothes. Everything else in these two emails — groups out of the gate, host pairs, what happens after — is aimed at relationships. This one is aimed at the door count.

**The narrower objection:** setting strategy aside, posing a question eight hours earlier is not a change anybody in the room can feel. It doesn't earn two of our eight weeks. *(Note for our own records: the vault has been calling this a "two-day arc." It isn't — John's version is same-day, morning to evening. Worth not repeating in front of him.)*

**The salvage, if John still wants it:** decouple it from the morning platform. Pose the question by text, social, and the app during the week — so it reaches people who were never in the room at 9 AM. That version is a reach mechanic instead of a repeat mechanic, and it's worth trying later.

> [!question] The question actually worth an hour of this meeting
> **Who is Sunday night for — people already at Grace on Sunday morning, or people who aren't?**
> We have been answering that differently in different rooms. It decides more than this one idea: it decides whether the marketing points inward or outward, whether "same message as the morning" is a feature or a liability, and what we're even measuring for eight weeks. Better settled today than in October.
