# Grace Brand Guideline — Summer 2019

**This is the brand standard.** It is the document [[Reference/Grace-Brand-Colors]] spent two pages asking for. Heather Noble owns it — the book names her as the contact on its final page (`heather.noble@trygrace.org`).

**File:** `Reference/GraceCommunity_BrandGuideline_190627.pdf` · 34 pages · `190627` = 2019-06-27.

> ⚠️ **It is from Summer 2019.** Seven years old, and Grace is building a new AI-agentic website (~late Oct 2026). It is authoritative until Heather says otherwise — but confirm with her before a large print run, and re-check once the new site ships.

## 🎨 The palette — four colors, and that is the whole palette

Every swatch below is given in the book in CMYK, RGB, hex **and** Pantone, and all four agree on every one. There is no fifth color and no tint system specified.

| Name | Hex | RGB | CMYK | Pantone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Active Blue** | **`#2F97A0`** | 47 / 151 / 160 | C77 M23 Y36 K1 | **PANTONE 2235 C** |
| **Stable Blue** | **`#1C3B6B`** | 28 / 59 / 107 | C100 M85 Y31 K18 | **PANTONE 534 C** |
| **Cinder Gray** | **`#ABABAB`** | 171 / 171 / 171 | C34 M27 Y28 K0 | **PANTONE 429 C** |
| **Cloud Gray** | **`#E3E3E3`** | 227 / 227 / 227 | C10 M7 Y7 K0 | **PANTONE 427 C** |

Note the names: the teal is called **"Active Blue"**, not a teal or a green. The navy is **"Stable Blue."** Active and stable — the pair is doing deliberate work, and it is worth using them that way rather than treating the navy as a dark neutral.

### 🔴 This reverses what the vault previously believed

[[Reference/Grace-Brand-Colors]] concluded, on 2026-08-18, that the website's `#2A95A0` was *"the only defensible teal"* and that Heather's promo deck's `#2F97A0` and `#1C3B6B` had *"no traceable Grace origin"* — with the deck's navy called out as possibly *"a generator inventing a churchy navy."*

**That was backwards, and the correction matters:**

| Value | 8/18 verdict | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| `#2F97A0` | "off by (5,2,0), no provenance" | ✅ **Active Blue. PANTONE 2235 C. The brand teal.** |
| `#1C3B6B` | "no traceable Grace origin" | ✅ **Stable Blue. PANTONE 534 C.** |
| `#ABABAB` | "deck footer line" | ✅ **Cinder Gray. PANTONE 429 C.** |
| `#E3E3E3` | "deck card fill" | ✅ **Cloud Gray. PANTONE 427 C.** |
| `#2A95A0` | "the brand color — use this" | ❌ **The website is the drift.** Off-book by (5,2,0). |

**Heather's promo deck was on-brand the whole time — all six of its colors come straight out of this book.** The suspicion fell on the deck because it was generated by PptxGenJS, and "generated" got read as "invented." It was the *website* that was off the standard, and the vault trusted the website because it could read its CSS.

The lesson is the one the old note was itself reaching for, just pointed the wrong way: **a value you can query is not the same as a value that is authoritative.** Ease of access is not provenance.

> Three Grace teals were in circulation: `#2F97A0` (book), `#2A95A0` (website), `#44979D` (7/26 volunteer sign). Only the first is real. The other two are drift and should be corrected on contact, not propagated.

## 🔤 Typography

**Specified faces — both licensed, neither free:**

| Role | Face |
|---|---|
| Display | **Brandon Grotesque Black** *(printed "GROSTEQUE" in the book — a typo in the source)* |
| Secondary | **Franklin Gothic Heavy** |
| Pull quotes | **Lato Black** — named explicitly in the type-hierarchy spec |

**Lato is the one brand-sanctioned face that is freely available** (Google Fonts, full Light→Black range). For web, artifacts and anything where Brandon Grotesque is not licensed, **build on Lato** and say so — it is a substitution the book itself half-authorizes rather than an invented choice.

### Type hierarchy, as specified

| | Size | Weight | Leading | Tracking |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| **Main Header** | 47pt | Light | — | 0 |
| **Sub-Header** | 15pt | Heavy | — | **160** |
| **Pull-Quote** | 11pt | Black | 15pt | **140** |
| **Body Copy** | 10pt | Regular | 13pt | 0 |

The signature move is the **very wide tracking on sub-heads and pull quotes** (160 and 140 — roughly `0.16em` and `0.14em`) against **untracked body copy**, with a **Light** main header rather than a bold one. Headers are light and quiet; the small type is heavy and spaced out. Reproduce that contrast and the page reads as Grace; use a bold header and tight caps and it will not, whatever the hex values say.

## 🧭 Brand story — the strategic identity

> *"A brand is an intangible idea that exists in the minds of people."*

Five attributes, each stated as a fact about the congregation that then implies something about the brand:

| We are… | …so the brand is |
|---|---|
| **DIVERSE** — physically diverse | inclusive and multi-cultural |
| **COURAGEOUS** — emotionally courageous | daring and faith-filled |
| **HUMBLE** — intellectually humble | genuine and authentic |
| **ACCEPTING** — socially accepting | inviting and non-judgmental |
| **UNEXPECTED** — spiritually unexpected | unconventional and surprising |

**"Intellectually humble → genuine and authentic" is the one to keep in view for Sunday Night.** The whole Q&A mechanic — anonymous submission, questions read aloud whether or not they are comfortable, a screener whose only filter is slurs and names — is that attribute expressed as a service format. It is on-brand by the book's own definition, which is a useful thing to be able to say to a room.

## Still open

- **The front-door decal, week of 9/13** needed a non-white color because white is unreadable in daylight. The book specifies no alternate reversed treatment — that is a p19 restrictions question for Heather.
- **Sunday Night has no sub-brand in this book.** Grace Women, Nextstep, Grace in 5 and Passion each got one in 2019. If Sunday Night Service is going to be a standing fourth service rather than an event, it eventually needs the same treatment — Heather's call, not a task.
- **Nothing here covers digital/UI tokens** — no hover states, no dark mode, no data-visualization guidance. Those have to be derived from the four colors, and derivations should be documented wherever they are made rather than invented per project.
