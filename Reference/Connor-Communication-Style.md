# Connor's Communication Style

Use this when drafting email, texts, or PCO/volunteer copy **as Connor**. The Copilot guide described one register (structured business email). The record shows several, and the Copilot rules fit only some of them. Pick the register by audience first.

## The through-line (all registers)

- **Lead with the point, then the ask.** First line is the greeting, then the request or answer.
- **Decisive, not pushy.** States a view and the reason: "I think we're going to need to simplify the attendance for this."
- **Warm by default.** "!" is normal, "Thanks!" ends most messages, an occasional emoji.
- **Specific over vague.** Names the date, time, link, owner, number. "Call time is 5:20, in time for our 5:30 volunteer huddle."
- **Makes the ask easy to say yes to.** Offers a fallback or removes pressure: "If not, I can get away with sending my visuals to someone who does have access." / "No rush if it's just been buried."
- **Gives the reason after the ask, labeled.** "The reason for asking:" then the logic, then the deadline math ("So we want to circulate the Q&A link starting 9/6 at the latest").
- **Plain words.** No consultant-speak.
- **Self-aware humor, including about AI.** He credits Claude openly and lightly ("Shoutout Claude for these notes", "I blame Claude!", "That was all Claude 🥴").

## Register 1: Staff email (supervisors, peers, admin)

**Shape:** greeting → one tight paragraph (or a few unlabeled question lines) → "Thanks," → "Connor". Replies are often one to three lines. About one in six sent items is a calendar acceptance, Zoom invite, or bare "FYI" forward.

- **Greetings by audience:** "Hey Kay -", "Hey Heather!", "Hey Debbie!" (individuals); "Hey Guys -", "Morning Guys,", "Morning Gents,", "Hey Gang,", "Gang!" (groups). Occasionally playful: "Ahoy Heather!", "Howdy!!".
- **Closers:** "Thanks!" / "Thanks," + "Connor" is the default. "Thank you thank you thank you!!" for real gratitude. "Thank you both <3<3<3" and "C" as the sign-off only with colleagues he's close to. "Warmly," to Heather after a favor.
- **Asks as direct questions:** "Does 10am work for you?" / "Let's do 4pm then?" / "Will it be on zoom?" / "Connect? Discuss? Chat?" Fragments are fine when the thread already has context.
- **Availability stated as windows:** "My best windows are 7am-9am and 3pm-5pm, but I am flexible." / "free after 1PM today and all day Friday."
- **Follow-ups are plain and sometimes light:** "Bump:)" / "Following up on my 9/3 note…" / "Just trying to close the loop since I took it as an open item from that discussion. Is this still open?"
- **Prefix for non-urgent:** subject lines like "Low priority" and "Low Priority - Drop Box Membership" so the reader can triage.
- **Approvals/forwards are bare:** "Approved! Billing code 7400", "FYI", "Fyi done".
- **Options with a pick:** "Three options, cheapest to priciest: … **My pick:** least mess and least cost." Then name-by-name asks: "**Jen** — what vegetarian mix do you usually assume? **Derek** — does $12/head work?"
- **Pushback = state fact, ask if there's a reason:** "Worship for the 4th service will not be the same as the morning services… Let me know if there's a reason."
- **Short apology + reason + proposal:** "Sorry for the short notice. … Can we have our QR code discussion later this week?"
- **Praises specifically, then adds a future idea:** "I like the day of schedule by owner… A future feature might include a tab for each role."
- **Self-notes to his own inbox:** one-line fragments ("Host on event brite / Share Partiful / Announce next week", "People want cookies"). Not a drafting model, but they show how he thinks in fragments.

**Structured emails** (team calls, planning recaps) are where the Copilot shape holds:
- Context line + constraint: "Team - Thanks for a part of your evening. 30 minutes, hard stop."
- Labeled blocks: "What this call is for:", "Agenda:", "Also:", "Proposed agenda (30 min):" with timestamps and numbered lists.
- Models the behavior he wants: "Come with four answers - mine as examples."
- Says what's **off** the agenda to keep it tight.
- "Current structure / Ideal structure" with `->` arrows for process proposals.
- Action items by owner in bold ("**Brian** — …", "**Derek** — …", "**Connor** — …").

## Register 2: Volunteers, newcomers, external contacts

- **Greeting:** "Hi Victoria," / "Hi Mara," for people he doesn't know yet; "Hey Cortland!!" once there's a relationship. Group sends use "Hey There!!".
- **Invitations:** "So glad you're getting plugged in." → the invite with day/time → what makes it different → "be sure to say hi!!!" → "So glad you're with us."
- **Answers the volunteer's actual question, in order, short:** open with "Good question." then one sentence per role/option, then a personal nudge ("it is a spot where I could really use you"), a date, a promise ("I will follow up with call times"), and a closing ask: "Please let me know if ushering sounds like a fit for you."
- **Decline/cancel handling is gracious and brief:** "Aw man I'm sorry to hear that! … We'll miss ya, but we appreciate your enthusiasm to come and help." / "No worries at all and I appreciate your heart to want to make it work. Thanks bro!!" A kind no with the reason and a compliment: "I wish we were connected sooner… it's a little too late to change the plan."
- **Logistics sends:** "Here's what you need to know:" then one or two plain sentences. End with a low-friction contact line: "Any questions? Email, text or call me :)" plus phone number.
- **Vendors/support:** short and appreciative: "Fix confirmed - thank you for your prompt resolution!!!" Faith vocabulary appears with some contacts only ("God bless!", "In him,"), not as a default.
- **Role in the signature changed:** "Event Coordinator" (June–July) → "Sunday Night Service Coordinator" (August on). Outlook adds it; never type it.

## Register 3: Texts

**Measured on ~1,900 of his 2026 outbound texts (reactions excluded):**

| Trait | Finding |
|---|---|
| Length | Median **8 words**; mean ~15. Most are one line. |
| Exclamation | ~25% of messages |
| Question | ~25% of messages |
| Emoji | ~10% (🙃 😎 😄 😊 🫡 🥴 😵, for tone) |
| "haha" / "lol" | ~1% |
| Starts lowercase | ~7% |
| Multi-line | ~9% (line breaks, not paragraphs) |
| Em dash | <1% (he types " - " or "…" instead) |

**How it reads:**
- Short, plain, conversational: "Tomorrow works for me! 12pm?" / "Yes!" / "Not at all my friend we're lucky to be in your mind!"
- **Answers first, then context.** "I need to check if she is on call but otherwise yes." then the dates.
- Closes loops: "No need to respond", "Will lyk", "Confirm w John tho".
- Abbreviations are casual and sparing: "wknd", "lyk", "lmk", "tho", "w", "pls", "ty", "tn".
- **Family/friends:** playful, affectionate, teasing, emoji and ❤️ freely.
- **Close colleagues:** candid; reasons out loud, offers options, flags concerns directly ("I would like your thoughts on how to proceed…"), asks for what he needs ("When you get a min, before Sunday, can you…").
- **Group texts to volunteers/team:** names + energy ("Brandon and Will! Week 2 - let's go!!"), then exact logistics and the real content pasted word for word, numbered if discrete.
- Reacts (Loved / 🫡) for acknowledgements instead of sending "ok".

## A caution about this email sample

Some of the tidiest emails in Sent Items are almost certainly Claude-drafted or Claude-edited. Tells: em dashes ("Yes, actually — one thing", "Ask one — … Ask two —"), bolded owner names, "Here's a summary of where we landed" recaps, and some pasted blocks that still carry Claude's HTML styling. His **hand-typed** emails (phone signature, short, hyphens and "…" instead of em dashes) are shorter, looser, and more exclamatory. This is a hypothesis from patterns, not something confirmed; ask him if it matters. **Calibrate toward the hand-typed voice**, and use the structured style only when the content really is an agenda, recap, or option list.

## Where the Copilot guide fits and where it doesn't

| Copilot said | What the record shows |
|---|---|
| Closes with an explicit ask ("Please confirm / Please let me know") | **Half right.** He closes with an ask, but usually in a casual form ("Does 10am work?", "Let me know!", "LMK!!"). "Please let me know…" appears in volunteer- and vendor-facing asks. |
| Context → Key Points → Next Steps with headers | Only on team/agenda/recap emails. Most emails are 1–3 lines. |
| Phrases: "Key updates:", "Next steps:", "At a high level:" | Not seen. Instead: "What this call is for:", "Agenda:", "Also:", "The reason for asking:", "Here's what you need to know:", "Ask one / Ask two". |
| Avoid polite filler | He avoids *corporate* filler, but warmth is core: "So glad you're getting plugged in", "Thank you thank you thank you!!". |
| Confident, no hedging | True on decisions; he does soften with fallbacks and "No rush", which is courtesy, not weakness. |
| Assign owners per line | True on recaps and planning ("Brian —", "Derek —"), and name-by-name asks inside paragraphs. |
| Business-model vocabulary | Church-ministry vocabulary: Sunday Night, connect portion, QOTN, confidence monitor, huddle, plugged in. |

Still valid from Copilot: direct and explicit about next steps; collaborative "we/let's"; skimmable when long.

## Drafting checklist

1. Audience → register (Staff / Volunteer-external / Text).
2. Greeting: "Hey [first name]" if known, "Hi [first name]" if new, "Hey Guys/Gang" for groups.
3. Line one: the ask or answer. One thought per paragraph; blank line between.
4. Name the specific date/time/link/owner. Give availability as windows.
5. If asking something heavy, add the reason and an easy fallback.
6. Close with "Thanks!" or a direct answerable question; "Please let me know if…" for formal volunteer/vendor asks.
7. One emoji at most, only for warm contexts. Prefer "!" and "…" over em dashes.
8. Don't type a signature. Don't write a paragraph where a line works.
9. Texts: one thought per message, median under ten words; multi-line only for logistics or pasted content.
10. Pushback: fact first, then "Let me know if there's a reason" or an alternative.
