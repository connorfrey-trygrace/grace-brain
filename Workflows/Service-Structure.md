# Workflow: Service Structure (Morning vs. Sunday Night)

## Purpose
Defines the standard component structure for a Grace weekend service and how the **Sunday Night** service differs from a **Sunday Morning** service. Parallels the internal/external event framework: one defining distinction, then component-by-component differences.

## Defining Distinction
- **Morning** = the full standing machine. Every team active, max coverage, run by **volunteer captains** drawing on a self-sustaining ~125-person volunteer base. Like an **external** event, the draw is built in — people show up and serve by habit.
- **Night** = lean and intimate by design (Pastor John's "slower, more intimate" 6 PM model for young professionals, after St. Thomas NYC). Single-thread roles, no captain layer, a crew **built from the ground up** — structurally much closer to an **internal** event than to Sunday morning.

> The point isn't that night is a smaller morning. It's a different mode of gathering — closer to the early church breaking bread house to house (Acts 2:46) than the temple-courts crowd. Strip the machinery, not the ministry.

## Challenges / Decisions Before Launch
1. **Captain layer → single owner.** Connor is the default night captain. Fine for launch, but it makes you the single point of coverage — plan to raise up and entrust a second as the service stabilizes (Jethro, Exodus 18; 2 Tim 2:2).
2. **ProPresenter operator is the actual bottleneck** — not any of the things being cut. Lock this before worrying about the trim list.
3. **Door + officer ownership.** Cutting the security team is reasonable for a small crowd, but *someone* must own building/parking lock & door open-close. Either the one officer doubles up or a greeter does.
4. **Don't strip assimilation with the lunch.** Night is pitched as intimate for newcomers — Meet Grace + a clear next step matters *more* here, even without a lunch.
